1.0Recap: what you already know

Before RD Sharma gets going, it assumes you're solid on these from before — addition and subtraction of integers. A one-line refresher:

  • Same sign → add absolute values, keep the sign. (−4) + (−7) = −11
  • Different signs → subtract absolute values, take the sign of the bigger one. 9 + (−13) = −4
  • Subtracting b = adding the additive inverse of b. 5 − (−3) = 5 + 3 = 8

If this part still feels shaky, say so and we'll build a full addition/subtraction section before going further — RD Sharma itself assumes it's already solid.

1.1Multiplication of integers

RULE

The product of two integers of like signs is equal to the product of their absolute values (a positive result).

The product of two integers of opposite signs is equal to the additive inverse of the product of their absolute values (a negative result).

EXAMPLE 1 — Determine each product
(i) 12 × 7  (ii) (−15) × 8  (iii) (−25) × (−9)  (iv) 125 × (−8)
(i) Both positive → 12 × 7 = 84
(ii) Opposite signs → −(15 × 8) = −120
(iii) Both negative (like signs) → +(25 × 9) = 225
(iv) Opposite signs → −(125 × 8) = −1000
EXAMPLE 2 — Product of three or more integers
Find: (i) 3 × (−8) × 5  (ii) 9 × (−3) × (−6)  (iii) (−2) × (−4) × (−6) × (−8)
(i) 3 × (−8) = −24, then −24 × 5 = −120
(ii) 9 × (−3) = −27, then −27 × (−6) = 162
(iii) Four negative factors (even count) → product is positive: 2 × 4 × 6 × 8 = 384
  • An even number of negative factors in a product → the product is positive.
  • An odd number of negative factors → the product is negative.
EXAMPLE 3 — using properties to simplify
Find the value of: 1487 × 327 + (−487) × 327
Both terms share a factor of 327 — factor it out (distributive property):
327 × [1487 + (−487)]
= 327 × 1000
= 327000

1.2Properties of multiplication of integers

  • Closure property: for any integers a, b — a × b is always an integer.
  • Commutative property: a × b = b × a
  • Associative property: (a × b) × c = a × (b × c)
  • Distributive property (over addition): a × (b + c) = a × b + a × c
  • Multiplicative identity: a × 1 = a = 1 × a
  • Multiplication by zero: a × 0 = 0 = 0 × a
  • Multiplication by (−1): a × (−1) = −a = (−1) × a — multiplying by −1 just flips the sign.
EXAMPLE 4 — find the integer
Determine the integer whose product with (−1) is: (i) 58  (ii) 0  (iii) −225
Multiplying by −1 flips the sign, so work backwards by flipping again.
(i) The integer is −58, since (−58) × (−1) = 58
(ii) The integer is 0, since 0 × (−1) = 0
(iii) The integer is 225, since 225 × (−1) = −225

1.3Division of integers

RULE

Division undoes multiplication, so the sign rules are identical: like signs give a positive quotient, opposite signs give a negative quotient.

EXAMPLE 5
Divide: (i) 102 by 17  (ii) −85 by 5  (iii) 0 by (−135)
(i) 102 ÷ 17 = 6
(ii) Opposite signs → −(85 ÷ 5) = −17
(iii) 0 divided by any non-zero integer is always 0.

1.4Properties of division of integers

  • NOT closed: a ÷ b is not always an integer, e.g. 1 ÷ 2 = 0.5
  • NOT commutative: a ÷ b ≠ b ÷ a in general
  • NOT associative: a ÷ (b ÷ c) ≠ (a ÷ b) ÷ c in general
  • a ÷ 1 = a for every integer a
  • a ÷ a = 1 for every non-zero integer a
  • a ÷ 0 is never defined; 0 ÷ a = 0 for any non-zero a

1.5Order of operations — BODMAS

When an expression has multiple operations with no brackets, RD Sharma uses the BODMAS order strictly: Division and Multiplication (left to right, whichever comes first), then Addition and Subtraction (left to right).

EXAMPLE 6
Find the value of: 36 ÷ 6 + 3
Division first: 36 ÷ 6 = 6
Then addition: 6 + 3 = 9
EXAMPLE 7
Find the value of: (−3) × (−4) ÷ (−2) + (−1)
Division first (left to right): (−4) ÷ (−2) = 2
Then multiplication: (−3) × 2 = −6
Then addition: (−6) + (−1) = −7

1.6Simplifying expressions with brackets

RD Sharma introduces one extra symbol here: the vinculum — a bar drawn over a group of numbers, like 7‾−5‾. It works exactly like an invisible bracket: whatever is under the bar gets simplified before anything else, even before round brackets.

ORDER FOR REMOVING GROUPING SYMBOLS

Vinculum → small brackets ( ) → curly brackets { } → square brackets [ ] — and within each type, always work from the innermost bracket outward.

EXAMPLE 8
Simplify: 3 − (5 − 6 ÷ 3)
Inside the bracket, division comes first: 6 ÷ 3 = 2
3 − (5 − 2) = 3 − 3
= 0
EXAMPLE 9 — nested brackets, working outward
Simplify: 27 − [38 − {46 − (15 − 11)}]
Innermost first: (15 − 11) = 4
27 − [38 − {46 − 4}]
Curly bracket next: {46 − 4} = 42
27 − [38 − 42]
Square bracket: [38 − 42] = −4
27 − (−4) = 27 + 4 = 31
EXAMPLE 10 — with division inside nested brackets
Simplify: 45 − [38 − {60 ÷ 3 − (6 − 9 ÷ 3) ÷ 3}]
Innermost bracket: 9 ÷ 3 = 3, so (6 − 3) = 3
45 − [38 − {60 ÷ 3 − 3 ÷ 3}]
Do the divisions inside the curly bracket: 60 ÷ 3 = 20, and 3 ÷ 3 = 1
45 − [38 − {20 − 1}] = 45 − [38 − 19]
45 − 19 = 26

Chapter 1 summary

  • Like signs multiply/divide to a positive result; opposite signs give a negative result.
  • Even count of negative factors → positive product; odd count → negative product.
  • Multiplication is closed, commutative, associative, and distributes over addition; 1 is the multiplicative identity.
  • Division is NOT closed, NOT commutative, NOT associative. Division by 0 is never defined.
  • BODMAS with no brackets: division/multiplication (left to right) before addition/subtraction (left to right).
  • With brackets: vinculum first, then ( ), then { }, then [ ] — innermost first within each.
